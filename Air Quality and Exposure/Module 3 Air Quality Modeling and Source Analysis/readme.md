# NextAIRE – Air Quality Modelling

**Next Generation AI Researchers for Air Quality Excellence**

## About the course

This course introduces the fundamentals of air quality modelling, covering air pollution sources, atmospheric processes and different approaches used to simulate the transport and dispersion of air pollutants.

It combines theoretical concepts with practical exercises, providing participants with an understanding of how air quality models work and how they can be applied in environmental research and assessment.

## What will you learn?

By completing this course, participants will:
- Understand the main sources and types of air pollutants.
- Learn about atmospheric transport, diffusion, chemical transformation and deposition.
- Understand the influence of meteorological conditions on air quality.
- Become familiar with Gaussian, Eulerian and Lagrangian models.
- Learn the basic principles and applications of air quality modelling.
- Perform and interpret air parcel trajectory simulations using NOAA HYSPLIT.

## Course content

The course consists of five main sections:

1. **Introduction to Air Quality Modelling** – Air quality, pollution sources, environmental impacts and the role of modelling.
2. **Air Pollutants** – Primary and secondary pollutants, major air pollutants and emerging contaminants.
3. **Atmospheric Processes** – Transport, diffusion, chemical transformations, deposition and meteorological influences.
4. **Air Quality Models** – Model building blocks, Gaussian, Eulerian and Lagrangian modelling approaches.
5. **Practical Exercises** – Hands-on work with NOAA HYSPLIT.

## Practical exercises – NOAA HYSPLIT

Participants will complete three exercises using the online NOAA HYSPLIT model:

- **Forward trajectory:** Investigating the transport of air masses from the Eyjafjallajökull volcano in Iceland.
- **Backward trajectory:** Identifying the potential origin of an air parcel arriving at the participant's residential location.
- **Dust intrusion:** Investigating the origin of a dust intrusion event and determining whether it originates from the Sahara Desert or another region.

## Tools and materials

- **NOAA HYSPLIT:** Online atmospheric trajectory modelling tool.
- **GFS meteorological data:** Input data for trajectory simulations.
- **Course script and presentation:** Supporting theoretical explanations and practical instructions.

HYSPLIT is available online, and the trajectory exercises do not require software installation or registration.

**HYSPLIT:** https://www.ready.noaa.gov/HYSPLIT.php
